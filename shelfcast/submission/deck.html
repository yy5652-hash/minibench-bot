The problem

How many should we order?

  • Every week, every store, every product. Too few is an empty shelf; too many is waste.
  • Replenishment systems extrapolate last month. They cannot see a holiday, a promotion, or what the store manager knows.
  • Planners patch the number by hand: slow, inconsistent, and never checked against what sold.

How it works

A statistical forecast, then judgment, then a decision.

1
Baseline
Exponential smoothing with quantiles from its own past errors.
2
Evidence
Past event and promotion effects, kept only if they clear the product's own noise.
3 · on AMD
LLM judgment
Reads the evidence and the planner's notes; returns a multiplier, two spreads, and reasons.
4
Guard + calibration
No evidence and no note: the baseline stands. Ranges recalibrated on past errors.
5
Order
The demand quantile where lost margin balances leftover cost.

The language model is never the only forecasting method.

What we learned, and what is next

We ran the ablations before the pitch.

  • Left free, the model made forecasts worse on real data: it read routine calendar entries as events. So it is the judgment layer, tied to evidence by a guard.
  • Its own contribution is measured apart from everything else, against a rule that uses the same evidence.
  • Limits: one week ahead, no carry-over stock, assumed margins.
  • Next: multi-week horizons, real promotion calendars, planner feedback as training data.
